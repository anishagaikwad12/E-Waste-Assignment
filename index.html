E-Waste Management

Understanding electronic waste, its impact, and how Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) helps us recycle responsibly.

Anisha Anil GaikwadRoll No: 25102A2010Topic: E-Waste

What is E-Waste?

E-waste is any discarded electrical or electronic equipment, such as old phones, computers, batteries, chargers and household appliances. It contains valuable materials like copper and gold, but also harmful substances such as lead, mercury and cadmium that can pollute soil and water if disposed of improperly.

Common Categories

IT & Telecom

Laptops, desktops, monitors, mobile phones, routers and printers.

Consumer Electronics

Televisions, speakers, cameras, music players and gaming consoles.

Home Appliances

Refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners and microwaves.

Batteries & Lighting

Lithium-ion batteries, power banks, CFLs and LED lamps.

Why It Matters

Environment

Toxic metals leach into soil and groundwater; burning cables releases harmful fumes.

Health

Informal recycling exposes workers to hazardous chemicals without protection.

Resources

Recycling recovers precious metals and reduces the need for new mining.

Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)

Under EPR, producers, manufacturers and importers are responsible for the collection and environmentally sound recycling of the electronics they place on the market. In India this is governed by the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, which set recycling targets and require registration of producers, recyclers and refurbishers on a central portal.

Reduce

Buy only what you need and choose durable products.

Reuse

Repair, refurbish, donate or resell working devices.

Recycle

Hand over devices to authorised collection centres and recyclers.
